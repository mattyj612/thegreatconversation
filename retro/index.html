The Great Conversation

a lecture by Matthew Jordan
May 27, 2026 Isabel Bader Theatre Toronto

Summary

On May 27th we sold out the Isabel Bader Theatre in Toronto for The Great Conversation. Our aims for the evening were to:

  • Assemble the best Toronto technologists to collectively recognize an emerging scene with a unique perspective on technology.
  • Highlight the best new publishers and media outlets in the world of technology, affirming the importance of ideas as the drivers of technological progress.
  • Work with mission-aligned companies that believe that to be a technologist is to place oneself in a historic lineage, and who aim to supplement, never replace, human creativity.

It was a resounding success.

Our Guests

The theatre was packed with 497 people, including engineers, data scientists, writers, policymakers, venture capitalists, and artists.

The audience had a near-even gender split, and ages ranged from young founders to emeritus professors. One fifth of attendees were students or recent graduates from the University of Toronto and the University of Waterloo. The most represented career (70 people) was AI/ML.

Dozens of founders were in the room, across industries including Xona Space Systems, GPTZero, AccessNow, Convictional, Rubric Labs and more.

Friends and co-conspirators held watch parties in 6 countries and 10 cities around the world, including every major US metro and a special viewing in the House of Commons for the federal government of Canada.

The Lecture & Panel

The centerpiece of The Great Conversation was Matthew's lecture. The argument: technology is not simply tools and devices; it's a dialogue about how the world ought to be. Technology is the actualization of ideas enacted by communities of practice, in small local scenes, transmitting specialized knowledge face-to-face.

Using case studies from Canadian technology history, the lecture urged the audience to understand themselves as part of something bigger than themselves. We are all in dialogue with a tradition of thinkers and builders who precede us. It is our responsibility to continue their work.

The lecture was followed by a panel from all four organizers where we discussed our efforts as community-builders to contribute to The Great Conversation.

The final Q&A touched on many related themes: if knowledge is in builders and not their tools, how should we think about AI? Why do we choose to invest so deeply in Toronto?

After the lecture and panel, over 150 people continued the conversation at a nearby pub. The majority stayed until close at 2 a.m.

The Book Fair

The Great Conversation is the first event to bring together the most exciting new media companies shaping the global conversation about technology: Colossus, Stripe Press, Asterisk, and Orbital Studies.

This part of the event is going to become even more important in future years; The Great Conversation will soon become the best book fair in tech.

The Response

The event resonated deeply with the audience. Many said it was the signature night of Toronto Tech Week. It gave them a new perspective on their role as technologists, and renewed their motivation to pursue meaningful work.

One theme stood out above the rest: knowledge is embedded in social relationships and passed face-to-face. Books and artifacts are useful as storage mechanisms, but living knowledge lies in people, and follows them where they go. Knowledge is in the builders, not the buildings.

Our Sponsors

The Great Conversation's sponsors are leaders in creating both extraordinary tools and transformative ideas. They embody the thesis that technologists are historical actors.

The evening began with testimonials and videos from our lead sponsors: Notion, Cursor, and Ambition. Ambrook, Colossus, and Positive Sum Ventures also participated in the book fair. These partnerships will have ripple effects for many years to come.

The week prior to The Great Conversation, we hosted a walking tour around Toronto to introduce them to the people, ideas, and institutions that are driving our city forward.
